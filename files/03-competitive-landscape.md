# Practice OS — Competitive Landscape

## Market Structure
The market bifurcated badly into three tiers, leaving a clear gap for a solo-focused business layer tool.

---

## Tier 1: EHR / Practice Management (Clinical Ops Layer)
Tools built for compliance and billing — not practice growth.

| Tool | Price | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|
| SimplePractice | $69–99/mo | Market leader, solo-friendly UX but clinically focused |
| TherapyNotes | $49/mo | Strong on billing, zero API access (closed ecosystem) |
| TheraNest | $39+/mo | Older UI, group-practice roots |
| Upheal | $69/mo | EHR + built-in AI notes, newer entrant |
| Sessions Health | Free–$29/mo | Newer, lightweight, gaining traction |

**Why they don't solve the problem:** Built for insurance billing workflow. No business intelligence. TherapyNotes has zero API — cannot connect to any external system. SimplePractice has some reporting but no proactive alerts or coaching. Language is clinical, not business.

---

## Tier 2: AI Notes Bolt-Ons (Documentation Layer)
All competing on the same axis — SOAP/DAP note quality, price per note, HIPAA compliance.

| Tool | Price | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|
| Mentalyc | $40–70/mo | Strong note quality, 100 notes/mo at $40 |
| Upheal AI | Included in EHR | Built into Upheal's platform |
| Freed | $99/mo | Popular with physicians too |
| AutoNotes | $18–49/mo | Budget option |
| Supanote | $49/mo | Unlimited notes |
| Blueprint | $79+/mo | Adds treatment planning |

**Why they don't solve the problem:** Single-purpose tools. Documentation time → saved. Practice health → zero visibility. Actively converging to commodity. Price wars have started.

---

## Tier 3: CRM / Growth Tools (Business Layer)
Closest to the gap — but built for group practices, not solo.

| Tool | Price | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|
| Therapy Flow | $200+/mo | CRM + marketing automation, built for group practices |
| Admirra | Demo-only pricing | Referral tracking, pipeline — designed for multi-clinician |
| Breksey | Not public | Marketing-focused |
| TheraSaaS | Varies | Practice growth tooling |

**Why they don't solve the problem:** Pricing ($200–500/mo) is calibrated for group practices with multiple revenue streams. Feature set is overkill for solo. "Pipeline" and "conversion" language is alienating to a therapist who never thinks of clients as leads.

---

## The Gap
Nobody owns this combination **for a solo therapist specifically**:
1. **Caseload intelligence** — dropout risk signals, real capacity visibility
2. **Retention-first client management** — not a sales CRM, but a care continuity layer
3. **Financial clarity** — effective hourly rate, utilization trends, "raise your rate" prompts
4. **Solo-appropriate pricing** — $49–79/mo, not $200+

## Moat Logic
- EHRs won't build this — it's outside their compliance-first worldview and would require opening their closed APIs
- AI notes tools won't build this — they're fighting a commodity war on their current axis
- Group-practice CRMs won't go downmarket — their product and pricing is architected for bigger customers
- This requires understanding how a solo therapist *thinks about their business*, which is a distinct design challenge

## Key Insight on Language
Therapists don't think in "pipeline", "leads", or "churn." The language that works:
- "Who might be pulling back?" (not "churn risk")
- "Is your schedule healthy?" (not "capacity utilization")
- "You're leaving $X on the table" (direct, but in service of the therapist's wellbeing)
- "Your practice this month" (not "your revenue dashboard")
