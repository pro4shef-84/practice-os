What your office manager handles

Three moments in your day your office manager owns — completely.

📋 When a new client is added

Your office manager sends the paperwork
before you ever meet them.

You add a name, email, and phone. Practice OS sends an intake link. They sign all five documents on their phone — consent forms, cancellation policy, intake questionnaire — and enter their card. By the time you sit down together, it's all done.

No emailing PDFs. No DocuSign. No chasing. The link expires after 72 hours. If they haven't finished, resend it in one click.

🛡 When a client cancels last minute

Your office manager has
the conversation you don't want to have.

A client cancels 3 hours before their session — inside your 24-hour window. You don't text them. You don't send an invoice. You don't have to decide whether to enforce the policy today.

Practice OS checks your policy, charges the late cancel fee to the card on file, and sends them a message. You get a notification. It's over. If you want to waive it, you can — but you never have to ask for it.

"I do emotionally demanding work all day. The absolute last thing I have capacity for at 6pm is chasing a client for $100. Now I never have to. It just happens, and I don't have to be the bad guy." — LPC, solo practice, Austin TX

📝 When a session ends

Your office manager closes out every session
while you're still in it.

Open the note, pick your template — SOAP, DAP, BIRP, or Progress Note — and start writing. Every keystroke is saved as you type. There is no save button. There is no "are you sure you want to leave?" There is no lost note on a Friday evening.

When you're done, click "Sign and lock." Mark the session complete. CPT code and diagnosis are pre-filled from the client's record. One more click sends the superbill directly to the client's email.

"I had six sessions on a Friday. Lost a 40-minute progress note at 6pm because I forgot to hit save. I rewrote it from memory at 8pm. That was the last time. Practice OS saves after every single keystroke — there's nothing to forget." — MFT, solo practice, Chicago IL
